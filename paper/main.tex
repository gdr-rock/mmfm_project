\begin{abstract}
Procedural video planning requires more than predicting the next action token: a model should understand why a step is chosen and what state transition it causes. We explore this by reproducing and expanding upon Vision-Language World Models (VLWMs). A significant obstacle was the lack of open-source Tree-of-Caption data from prior works. To address this, we developed a synthetic pipeline utilizing COIN and CrossTask annotations to replicate goal, action, state-change, and critic data. We evaluated Perception-LM variants (1B LoRA vs. 8B GaLore) under constrained compute settings. Crucially, we incorporate V-JEPA latent features to ground state changes in actual physical representations, overcoming limitations in purely text-synthesized environments. Our evaluations show that while the plain 1B model suffers heavily from textual hallucinations, providing goal interpretations and scaling to the 8B grounded model yields a significant boost in planning accuracy (Step F1 soaring from 0.01 to $\sim$0.39). We analyze failure modes, expose prior flaws related to dataset corruption and data leakage, and propose continuous video-context integration as vital future work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Many procedural tasks require causal reasoning over intermediate world states, not only action imitation. In long-horizon instructional videos, the same verb can represent very different decisions depending on current progress toward the goal. This motivates our core research question: \emph{Does explicit state-transition supervision, grounded in physical video latents, improve procedural planning quality compared to action-only decoding under matched training conditions?}

Our work is framed by a practical constraint: the full Vision-Language World Model (VLWM) ecosystem, particularly the rich Tree-of-Caption dataset used in state-of-the-art papers, is not publicly available. Instead of being deterred, we treat this as a research parameter limit. We reconstruct the dataset pipeline by mapping available COIN and CrossTask datasets into a System-1 (generation), Critic (scoring), and Goal Latent format. However, relying purely on text for visual tasks leads to severe domain disparities. To bridge this, we incorporate \textbf{V-JEPA (Video Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture)} latents, which strictly enforce that a model's proposed ``state change'' matches real-world temporal physics.

The primary contributions of this work are: (1) a comprehensive synthetic data generation and replication methodology for VLWMs; (2) an empirical evaluation indicating how foundational scale (8B GaLore) and explicit visual latents (V-JEPA) reduce hallucinations compared to a plain 1B baseline; and (3) an architectural exploration into scoring plans via combined Critic and Goal-Energy functions. 

\section{Related Work}
Procedural understanding benchmarks such as COIN \citep{tang2019coin} and CrossTask \citep{zhukov2019crossTask} establish that temporally ordered actions alone are insufficient for robust task reasoning. They provide an important dataset substrate for evaluating task progression over local token patterns.

Recent advances in Vision-Language World Models pose that planning quality heavily depends on representations of transition dynamics. Specifically, predictive learning works like V-JEPA \citep{assran2025vjepa2} shift the paradigm from pixel reconstruction to predicting higher-level conceptual latents of a video. In planning-oriented VLMs \citep{chen2025planning}, the focus is modeling the consequences of an action before it is executed. Our position combines these themes: we validate the necessity of explicit delta-state tokens mapped to physical V-JEPA latents for effective planning without requiring the unavailable Tree-of-Caption data.

\section{Dataset Generation and Constraints}
Because the original open-source Tree-of-Caption dataset is unavailable, we engineered an end-to-end dataset generation pipeline. We utilized the raw JSON data of the COIN and CrossTask datasets and constructed formatted \texttt{action} and \texttt{state\_change} sequences. 

\textbf{Synthetic Pipeline:} The data was split to train three separate sub-systems:
\begin{enumerate}[topsep=0pt, itemsep=2pt, leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{System-1 Data:} Formatted as a serialized target combining $\texttt{GOAL\_DESCRIPTION}$, $\texttt{GOAL\_INTERPRETATION}$, and trajectory items $\langle A_t, \Delta S_t \rangle$.
    \item \textbf{Critic Data:} Generates contrastive examples of correct vs. incorrect future states to train an evaluator model.
    \item \textbf{Goal Latent Data:} Mapped final state textual representations to specific video checkpoints.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{V-JEPA Latent Extraction Challenges:} A major hurdle was acquiring the grounding frames. Many source videos from the original COIN dataset URLs were no longer available on YouTube due to link rot. Consequently, V-JEPA extraction could only be applied to a highly constrained subset of videos. We optimized around this by leveraging a 70/30 curriculum batching strategy (70\% text uniform, 30\% latent-primary) to learn structure despite sparse latent pairs.

\textbf{Controlling for Data Leakage:} In an earlier iteration of our experiments, the model performed suspiciously well. Upon deeper inspection, the evaluation tasks possessed exactly identical sequence steps as tasks in the training set, just applied to different video IDs. This corruption/leakage defeated the goal of zero-shot generalizability. To correct this, we instituted harsh dataset boundaries explicitly isolating task categories in our current testing harness.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Architecture Overview}
The model is composed of three interconnected modules acting together during inference:
\begin{itemize}[topsep=0pt, itemsep=2pt, leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{System-1 (Generator):} An autoregressive LLM (\texttt{Perception-LM}) trained to generate a sequence of paired actions and state changes based on a goal.
    \item \textbf{Critic Model:} A scoring model that evaluates the semantic likelihood of the generated sequence.
    \item \textbf{Goal Model:} A latent-space distance evaluator calculating the similarity/energy between the proposed final state and the initial objective.
\end{itemize}

During inference, System-1 generates $K$ candidate plans (e.g., $K=3$ using targeted temperature sampling). These candidates are passed to the Critic and Goal models. Their respective scores are normalized and combined into an energy function, allowing the system to rerank and pick the optimal planning path.

\subsection{Training Paradigms}
Our methodology involved optimizing models of different scales out of compute necessity. We utilized a SLURM cluster equipped with NVIDIA A40 GPUs, placing strict bounds on VRAM:
\begin{itemize}[topsep=0pt, itemsep=2pt, leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{1B LoRA:} We trained a \texttt{Perception-LM-1B} baseline using Low-Rank Adaptation ($r=16$, $\alpha=32$). This allowed for very rapid optimization ($\approx 1.85$ hours/epoch) but proved highly susceptible to semantic hallucinations.
    \item \textbf{8B GaLore:} To explore scaling laws, we applied Gradient Low-Rank Projection (GaLore, rank $256$) to \texttt{Perception-LM-8B}. While computationally intensive ($\approx 7$ hours/epoch), GaLore facilitated full-parameter updates within a single A40's 40GB limit by projecting gradients into a lower-dimensional space, preventing OOM errors.
\end{itemize}

We evaluated the models on clean COIN-2 test splits. The testing protocol required the model to output valid structured JSON sequences. We measured Exact Match and Step-F1.

Reranking generated candidates with the Critic and Goal model added an additional performance envelope.

\subsection{Qualitative Analysis and Hallucinations}
Inspection of the raw generation logs (JSON outputs) uncovers why the 1B models scored so poorly: extreme hallucinations. Without visual context or significant parameter scale, a purely textual 1B model defaults to associative text guessing.
For example, for the task \textit{``Paste Car Sticker''} with ground-truth steps \textit{[clean window $\rightarrow$ tear sticker $\rightarrow$ put sticker on]}, the 1B plain model output:
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\ttfamily\scriptsize, backgroundcolor=\color{gray!10}, frame=single]
{"action": "cut the raw materials", "state_change": "...",
 "action": "draw pictures on materials", "state_change": "...",
 "action": "put the candle in the lantern", "state_change": "..."}
\end{lstlisting}
The model completely hallucinates a separate task (making a paper lantern). V-JEPA spatial grounding attempts to fix this by matching states to visual realities, but at the 1B parameter count, the model lacks the capacity to bridge the abstraction gap effectively. 

\section{Discussion and Future Work}
\textbf{Metric Strictness:} While the models clearly struggled, metrics like Exact Match or discrete Step F1 are incredibly punishing. In one evaluation case where the goal was to \textit{``Change Car Tire,''} the model generated an entirely valid sequential response (\textit{``brake on $\rightarrow$ unscrew wheel $\rightarrow$ jack down''}) but scored an F1 of 0.000 because it did not strictly phrase it as \textit{``jack up the car''}. Future evaluations must leverage LLM-as-a-judge approaches for semantic validation rather than string equivalence.

\textbf{V-JEPA and Dataset Scaling:} Adding V-JEPA latents successfully introduces representational pressure without inflating runtimes, producing meaningful validation latent signals. However, because our training utilized solely pre-extracted latent states representing `checkpoints` alongside text, the model remains semi-blind. 

\textbf{Future Work:} To fully realize a Vision-Language World Model, the system should not rely merely on text goals and isolated checkpoint queries. The primary future objective is the continuous integration of raw video frame context via sliding multimodal windows. By embedding chronological vision tokens directly into the LLM context, the model can generalize to unseen states naturally, rather than treating planning as a blind textual serialization task. 

\section{Conclusion}
We constructed a constrained pipeline duplicating the behaviors of unavailable Tree-of-Caption structured datasets. We proved that pure-text 1B models default to severe hallucination (Step F1 0.01), whereas equipping an 8B GaLore model with grounded data, goal interpretations, and critic-energy reranking improves accuracy by orders of magnitude (Step F1 0.39). While the introduction of V-JEPA latents introduces essential physical anchoring, true dynamic generalization calls for incorporating end-to-end continuous video contexts in future iterations. The study highlights both the promise of structured causal training and the limitations of strict textual evaluation formats for continuous physical tasks.
