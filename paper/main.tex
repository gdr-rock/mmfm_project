\begin{abstract}
Procedural video planning requires a predictive understanding of how current actions transform an environment toward a defined goal. Recent work on Vision-Language World Models (VLWMs) aims to move beyond surface-level textual sequence generation by modeling grounded physical state transitions. In this work, we reproduce and extend core ideas from VLWMs by incorporating V-JEPA 2 (Video Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture) predictive latents into a multimodal reasoning pipeline. Our objective is to encourage linguistic action modeling to align with verifiable physical video transition spaces, with the goal of improving physical grounding and supporting generalization to previously unseen, zero-shot tasks.

To this end, we curate a procedural dataset from COIN and CrossTask annotations and organize it into a dual-system framework consisting of a System-1 Rollout Generator and a System-2 Scorer (Critic and Goal Model). We integrate V-JEPA latent features through an auxiliary projection head and apply dynamic energy-based heuristics during test-time reranking.

Our experimental findings reveal a clear contrast. While the model exhibits near-perfect task recreation and physical consistency on tasks seen during training, it struggles to generalize these grounded capabilities to previously unseen tasks in zero-shot settings. Moreover, we demonstrate that providing video-specific (instance-level) visual interpretations significantly improves task success rates compared to generic task-level interpretations. We further analyze the methodology, training dynamics under compute constraints, the impact of Critic-Goal reranking, and the reliance on global rather than local grounding. Finally, we highlight how rigid textual metrics (Task Success, Step F1, mIoU) can mask underlying semantic improvements in grounded planning.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Real-world intelligence depends on a causal understanding of the environment. For artificial agents, generating a sequence of procedural steps requires more than statistical word matching; it requires some degree of grounding in how the physical world changes over time. In long-horizon instructional settings, the meaning of an action depends strongly on its physical consequence and on how it contributes to the final state. For example, the action \textit{``add water''} implies very different physical trajectories in the tasks \textit{``making coffee''} and \textit{``mixing cement''}. This motivates our central research question: \emph{Does incorporating non-generative, predictive video latents into the representation space of an action-planning language model measurably improve its physical grounding and its ability to solve unknown tasks?}

Our work attempts to integrate the predictive representations of V-JEPA 2 \citep{assran2025vjepa2} into the procedural framework of Vision-Language World Models (VLWMs) \citep{chen2025planning}. Standard Large Language Models (LLMs) used for task planning are well known to produce physically implausible yet linguistically fluent plans. By default, purely textual models do not directly represent object appearance, material constraints, or physical dynamics. By tying the hidden states of an LLM to 1408-dimensional V-JEPA latent vectors extracted from video segments, we hypothesize that the model can function not only as a language generator but also as an approximate transition model over procedural states. At inference time, this design further enables a System-2 reranking framework that evaluates proposed action sequences using a latent \textit{energy score}, defined by the deviation between the predicted state and the target goal state vector.

Our architecture reconstructs the logic of proprietary VLWM pipelines while adapting it to open-source constraints. We investigate the viability of this predictive architecture under restricted compute and data constraints. Specifically, our analysis maps the failure points such as the \textit{grounding gap} that occur when attempting zero-shot physical generalization. A major obstacle in multimodal planning research remains the lack of access to the highly curated Tree-of-Caption datasets used by several state-of-the-art systems. Rather than treating this only as a limitation, we use it as an opportunity to build a synthetic dataset generation pipeline directly from the raw annotations of COIN \citep{tang2019coin} and CrossTask \citep{zhukov2019crossTask}. This pipeline reformats raw labels into explicit visual state transitions, $\langle A_t, \Delta S_t \rangle$, for System-1 trajectory rollout generation, and also supports the specialized splits required to train our Critic and Goal models.

Through model training across multiple scales from 1B-parameter LoRA variants to 8B-parameter GaLore implementations our findings show a consistent pattern. When evaluated on tasks that remain within the training distribution, the V-JEPA-augmented VLWM can closely recreate target procedures and align text and video latent representations. However, we observe a substantial drop when the same system is applied to zero-shot, out-of-distribution tasks. In these settings, the physical grounding encouraged by the auxiliary heads does not fully behave like a general-purpose physics simulator, and the system tends to fall back on linguistic memorization.

We analyze these shortcomings in detail, focusing on the effects of limited scale (roughly 3,000 videos versus proprietary datasets with millions of examples), the weaknesses of heuristic energy functions, and the difficulty of mapping continuous video dynamics onto discrete text tokens. Overall, the main contributions of this work are threefold: first, we map out the mechanics of a hybrid V-JEPA + language-model architecture for procedural planning; second, we construct a synthetic dataset reconstruction pipeline for COIN and CrossTask under open-source constraints; and third, we provide a transparent empirical analysis of the capabilities and limitations of test-time System-2 search for zero-shot spatial generalization in VLM-based planning.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Procedural Video Understanding:}
Early work on procedural video understanding focused primarily on dense video captioning and step localization. Benchmarks such as COIN \citep{tang2019coin} and CrossTask \citep{zhukov2019crossTask} moved the field beyond basic action recognition toward structured sequential reasoning. More recent work has shifted toward predicting future steps over time, effectively framing the task as planning rather than retrospective labeling.

\paragraph{Reasoning and Planning in Language Models:}
In NLP, planning has been explored through approaches such as Chain-of-Thought and Tree-of-Thoughts, where models simulate multiple candidate paths before committing to an answer. The broader System-1/System-2 perspective has also been instantiated computationally: a fast generator proposes candidate rollouts, while a slower evaluator scores their consistency or value. However, when such methods are applied directly to visually grounded tasks, they often encounter a persistent \textit{grounding gap}: generated steps may remain linguistically coherent while being physically implausible in the real world.

\paragraph{Predictive Video Architectures and VLWMs:}
The Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \citep{assran2025vjepa2} avoids pixel-level generation and instead predicts higher-level representation spaces. Vision-Language World Models (VLWMs) \citep{chen2025planning} build on this direction by attempting to combine language modeling and predictive video representations to anticipate the consequences of actions. Our work operates at the intersection of these ideas. Specifically, we investigate whether the inclusion of V-JEPA through an auxiliary projection head can inject useful transition structure into openly available perception-language architectures.

\section{Architecture and Methodology}

We adopt a dual-system paradigm that combines sequential text generation with test-time search guided by V-JEPA transition distances.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
Given a procedural task defined by an initial environmental observation frame $O_0$ (or a textual goal $G$) together with an end-goal description, the objective is to generate a valid sequence of actions $\{A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_T\}$ and their corresponding state changes $\{\Delta S_1, \Delta S_2, \ldots, \Delta S_T\}$. Standard decoding relies only on the conditional probability $P(A_t, \Delta S_t \mid O_0, G, A_{<t})$. Our formulation extends this by introducing a latent-space evaluation mechanism at inference time.

\subsection{System-1: The Rollout Generator}
System 1 is responsible for rapidly generating candidate future trajectories. We experimented with causal models based on \texttt{facebook/Perception-LM} variants ranging from 1B to 8B parameters.
The model receives as input the task goal description ($G$), an optional visual interpretation that maps $O_0$ into a textual prefix, and the history of preceding actions and state transitions. During inference, we apply high-temperature ($T=0.8$) nucleus sampling to produce $K$ diverse candidate action sequences.

\subsection{V-JEPA 2 Latent Projection Heads Setup}
The core architectural modification is the explicit inclusion of video-latent structure within the System-1 model. We use a \texttt{vjepa2} ViT-L configuration to process 16-frame slices of source videos, producing 1408-dimensional feature tensors that represent the underlying physical state transition.

To connect text outputs to video representations, we attach a 2-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) projection head to the final hidden-state layers of the System-1 language model. The goal is to map token embeddings associated with the state-change description ($\Delta S_t$) into the same 1408-dimensional space as the corresponding V-JEPA observation. During training, the total scalar loss is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{text-NLL} + \alpha \mathcal{L}_{latent}
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathcal{L}_{text-NLL}$ is the standard negative log-likelihood cross-entropy objective, while $\mathcal{L}_{latent}$ performs regression against the extracted V-JEPA vector. This encourages the language model's semantic representations to align with the predictive video representation space.

\subsection{System-2: Test-Time Reranking and Goal Critics}
System-1 generations can still hallucinate implausible intermediate steps. To mitigate this, System-2 performs test-time reranking over the generated candidates. We implemented two evaluators:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Evaluator Critic:} A classification model (incorporating a \texttt{sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2} encoder and an MLP head) trained via a pairwise max-margin ranking loss. It learns to score the validity of positive gold syntactic sequences against negative hallucinated cross-task rollouts, emitting a scalar score $Cost_{critic}$.
    \item \textbf{The Goal Latent Evaluator:} A sequence-to-vector regression model trained with Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss to map natural-language trajectory text directly into the exact 1408-dimensional V-JEPA continuous feature space. For each candidate textual plan, the text is passed through System-1 and the latent projection head to obtain a sequence of predicted continuous embeddings. The terminal trajectory latent $z_{pred\_final}$ is formed by aggregating the projected latent vectors of the generated action and state-change steps:
    \begin{equation}
        z_{pred\_final} = \sum_{t=1}^T z_{pred\_t}
    \end{equation}
    The textual goal $G$ is separately embedded by a dedicated Goal Model into $z_{goal}$.
\end{enumerate}

These components jointly define an overall energy score $E$:
\begin{equation}
   E_{total} = \alpha * Cost_{critic} + \beta * \|z_{pred\_final} - z_{goal}\|^2 
\end{equation}
The candidate sequence with the lowest total energy is selected as the final output.

\section{Dataset Generation and Synthetic Labeling}
A major bottleneck in this line of research is the absence of openly available datasets that closely match the proprietary resources used in state-of-the-art systems. To address this, we developed a data ingestion and reconstruction pipeline directly on top of the COIN and CrossTask datasets.

\subsection{Raw Video Processing}
While the original COIN dataset tracks annotations for 11,827 videos, widespread link rot affecting standard URLs restricted our processing. We successfully downloaded and extracted valid preprocessed representations for exactly 1,422 valid video sequences. These videos were divided into 16-frame temporal buffers and processed through the V-JEPA ViT-L model to generate foundational 1408-dimensional latent tensors.

\subsection{Structuring Target Responses}
We constructed a structured JSON format directly from the raw textual annotations and temporal boundaries provided by COIN and CrossTask. By mapping the original descriptions into explicit semantic forms, we defined both the action and its immediate resulting physical state change. The synthesized format, designed for the System-1 multimodal parser, follows the configuration below:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=json, caption={Sample Dataset Structure formatting State Changes}]
{
  "goal_description": "Change a car tire",
  "history": [
    {"action": "apply parking brake", "state_change": "car is secured"}
  ],
  "next_action": "loosen lug nuts",
  "next_state_change": "nuts are slightly unscrewed but wheel remains on"
}
\end{lstlisting}

To evaluate generalization on unknown procedural configurations, we enforced a strict multi-tier data split. Standard training pipelines often leak structurally similar tasks across train and evaluation subsets, which leads to inflated performance through task overlap. To minimize this effect, we applied a strict horizontal split: if a higher-level task was assigned to the evaluation partition, its step-level variants were excluded from the training corpus.

\section{Training Regimen and Hardware Capacity}

Our pipeline was executed on an academic hardware cluster. Training architectures up to 8B parameters on single GPUs made full-parameter fine-tuning infeasible, so we adopted memory-efficient adaptation strategies:
\begin{itemize}[topsep=0pt, leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{1B Parameter Variant via LoRA:} For smaller-scale experiments with a 1B model, we used Low-Rank Adaptation ($r=16, \alpha=32$). This substantially accelerated training, but ultimately limited the model's capacity to represent more complex semantic manifolds.
    \item \textbf{8B Parameter Variant via GaLore:} To increase capacity, we used Gradient Low-Rank Projection (GaLore) with an orthogonal rank of 256. This enabled an approximation closer to full-parameter updating without exceeding memory limits, while also reducing the risk of catastrophic forgetting often seen in more restrictive adaptation schemes.
\end{itemize}

During training, we used a curriculum strategy in which approximately 70\% of batches optimized the standard textual objective, while 30\% optimized the latent projection loss. This was intended to stabilize linguistic performance before enforcing stronger physical-grounding constraints.

We evaluated the pipeline using a standard task-planning setup, with particular attention to how the model responds to different prompt prefixes and reranking strategies.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Task Success Rate (SR):} Measures whether the generated sequence exactly matches the expected full sequence.

\end{enumerate}

The findings indicate that tuning the prompt to encompass the unique context of the target video (Video-Level) directly doubles the strict end-to-end exact completion metric (\textbf{Task Success}: 0.037 $\rightarrow$ 0.074). The model is better anchored to reality when generating the sequence. \subsection{Generalization Failures at Lower Scales (1B Representation Collapse)}
When evaluated on test sets composed of tasks already present in the training distribution, the models can reproduce procedures almost perfectly. However, performance degrades sharply when the same models are applied to entirely unknown procedural domains. 

Specifically, experiments using the 1B parameter variant across all prefix conditions yielded 0.000 task success and negligible F1 scores (e.g., maximum Step F1 of 0.033), indicating severe mode collapse. Qualitative inspection shows clear textual hallucinations---for example, when prompted with \textit{"Paste Car Sticker"}, the 1B model produced sequences more similar to kitchen recipes than to automotive maintenance. Attaching V-JEPA latent projection vectors does not meaningfully correct this behavior at the 1B scale. The smaller LLM lacks the representational capacity to form a joint embedding space between semantic language generation and 1408-dimensional continuous physical dynamics. Because generating a single physically plausible candidate fails fundamentally, System-2 reranking simply selects the "least bad" hallucination. Consequently, we explicitly present these 1B failures in Table~\ref{tab:evaluation} to illustrate the catastrophic collapse, before focusing our subsequent detailed analysis entirely on the 8B capacity.

\subsection{The Necessity of Scaled Capacity and Search}
Moving to the \texttt{8B GaLore} configuration substantially improves the baseline, indicating that a minimum capacity threshold is necessary for handling multimodal planning reliably. The 8B baseline under greedy decoding reaches a Step F1 of 0.128 on entirely unseen tasks.

When a targeted visual-interpreter prefix is added and System-2 reranking is enabled, performance increases sharply to 0.395 Step F1. Greater model capacity naturally reduces the variance of semantic hallucinations. Once the base generator produces more plausible candidates, the Critic and Goal-Latent Energy scorers become more effective at ranking them, penalizing omitted functional steps and favoring trajectories that lie closer to the target latent goal representation.

\section{Discussion and Case Analysis}

\subsection{Data Scale Discrepancies}
Our curated dataset relies on a restricted subset of 1,422 valid video mappings. This limited scale tightly restricts the density of the state-transition manifold. Under these data volume constraints, the continuous V-JEPA latent energy score serves as a weak patch rather than a complete generative solution. System-2 reranking can ultimately only select the best candidate from what System-1 generates; when System-1 cannot reliably generate physically plausible step rollouts for zero-shot tasks, the latent evaluator's impact remains marginal.

\subsection{Qualitative Analysis: Model Fallbacks \& Metric Stringency}
Upon qualitative inspection, we noticed that when the model encounters fully unseen dynamics, it resorts to linguistic memorization, producing plausible-sounding but sometimes flawed physical sequences. The V-JEPA System-2 evaluator penalizes severely deformed trajectories by minimizing the energy score against the target latent, selecting slightly more rational steps, albeit still imperfect.

Furthermore, exact-match stringency underestimates the model's underlying structural competence. A generated plan may reflect causal understanding while still being penalized for lexical variation. Consider the following representative rollout from an unknown task evaluation (where base auto-regressive decoding hallucinated, and the V-JEPA ranked generation captured the semantic mechanics better):

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent
\textbf{Input Goal:} \textit{Paste Car Sticker} \\
\textbf{Expected Label:} \textit{clean window surface $\rightarrow$ tear off front $\rightarrow$ put sticker on window $\rightarrow$ press sticker $\rightarrow$ tear off other side} \\
\textbf{1B Plain Model (Base):} \textit{pull out inner tube $\rightarrow$ insert new rubber sleeve $\rightarrow$ wrap tape $\rightarrow$ clean scratch} \\
\textbf{1B V-JEPA Grounded:} \textit{remove old rubber sleeve from wiper head $\rightarrow$ put new rubber sleeve $\rightarrow$ install wiper back} \\
\textbf{8B V-JEPA Grounded:} \textit{clean the window surface $\rightarrow$ wipe the cleaning agent $\rightarrow$ do not let the glue dry $\rightarrow$ put the sticker on the window $\rightarrow$ press the sticker}
\vspace{0.5em}

This side-by-side generation highlights the exact scale constraints. The base un-grounded 1B model hallucinated a mechanical task resembling tire-tube replacement. While the 1B V-JEPA grounded model remained fixated on automotive vocabulary (wiper replacement), it completely failed the specific instructions. Conversely, the scaled 8B Grounded model captured the nearly perfect mechanical progression---from surface cleaning to chemical adhesion and pressing. However, because it generated intermediate semantic phrases like \textit{"wipe the cleaning agent"} and \textit{"do not let the glue dry"}, the generated output receives an F1 score of \textbf{0.000} under strict heuristic evaluation. The System-2 reranker often favored this sequence because its continuous V-JEPA trajectory projection mapped closer to the goal state than alternatives. This proves that while the auxiliary V-JEPA constraint helps regularize semantics towards physical realism, rigid lexical evaluation protocols fail to reflect this latent improvement.

\subsection{Granularity of Visual Interpretation: Assumptions and Limitations}
A critical assumption in our current methodology is the temporal scope of the visual interpretation prefix. Currently, the entire video representation is compressed into a single global interpretation presented at the onset of planning ($O_0$). Rather than assuming a single visual summary could guide the language model organically through the entire step-by-step physical process, we used this static global configuration specifically to examine the comparative effect of varying initial semantic interpretations.

However, complex instructions require localized, step-by-step physical grounding. Because we haven't implemented per-action visual interpretation feedback during auto-regressive generation, the system maintains a "blind spot" as it steps deeper into the plan. If the model hallucinates at step $t=3$, there is no localized visual check to rein it in before step $t=4$; the Critic only scores the holistic trajectory at the very end. Because the open-loop terminal reranking only evaluates the holistic trajectory at the end, intermediate state drift is heavily penalized globally but not corrected locally. The most vital takeaway from this empirical failure is that future multimodal planning architectures must shift from initial global grounding and open-loop terminal trajectory ranking to \textbf{closed-loop, local state-transition interpretation} for each action sequence, ensuring continuous physical validation per step.

\section{Conclusion}
This study investigated whether introducing V-JEPA 2 latent physics awareness through an auxiliary projection architecture can improve procedural reasoning in Vision-Language models. By constructing a custom procedural pipeline under open-source constraints, we obtain a mixed picture. The architecture can faithfully reproduce tasks that remain within the training distribution and can align textual and latent representations under those conditions. However, substantial model scale (8B+) and test-time scoring remain necessary even to obtain partial stability on previously unseen zero-shot tasks.

Although the semantic grounding gap on out-of-distribution procedures remains unresolved---due in part to limited dataset scale and heuristic evaluation mechanisms---our implementation helps identify the practical thresholds and bottlenecks involved in combining predictive video representations with text generation. These findings clarify the architectural challenges that future work must address. Specifically, shifting from open-loop planning based on global interpretations to closed-loop, action-by-action local grounding will be critical for building robust multimodal world models capable of long-horizon procedural reasoning.
