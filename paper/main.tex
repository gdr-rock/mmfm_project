\begin{abstract}
Procedural video planning demands a predictive understanding of how current actions systematically alter environments toward a defined goal. Recent advancements in Vision-Language World Models (VLWMs) attempt to address this by moving beyond mere textual sequence generation into grounded physical state predictions. In this work, we reproduce and expand upon the core concepts of VLWMs by incorporating V-JEPA 2 (Video Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture) predictive latents into a multimodal reasoning pipeline. Our objective was to force linguistic action modeling to ground itself in verifiable physical video transition spaces, thereby enhancing the model's capability to generalize the learned physics to entirely unknown, zero-shot tasks. To achieve this, we curated a comprehensive procedural dataset from COIN and CrossTask annotations, formatting it into a dual-system paradigm involving a System-1 Rollout Generator and System-2 Scorer (Critic and Goal Model). We integrated V-JEPA latent features via an auxiliary projection head, injecting dynamic energy-score heuristics during test-time reranking. 

Our experimental findings reveal a distinct contrast: while the resulting model exhibits near-perfect task recreation and physical consistency on data it was explicitly trained on, it struggles to effectively generalize these grounded capabilities to previously unseen tasks. In this paper, we extensively analyze the methodology, the training dynamics constrained by compute and scale, the impact of Critic-Goal reranking over greedy decoding, and why our evaluation metrics expose the fundamental generalization gaps of purely heuristic-based text-to-latent world models.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Real-world intelligence relies intricately on a causal understanding of the environment. For artificial agents, generating a sequence of procedural steps requires more than statistical word-matching; it mandates a grounded comprehension of the physical world. In long-horizon instructional scenarios, the semantic meaning of an action heavily depends on its physical consequence and its advancement toward a final state. For example, the action \textit{``add water''} entails differing physical trajectories whether the task is \textit{``making coffee''} or \textit{``mixing cement''} This motivates our core research question: \emph{Does incorporating non-generative, predictive video latents into the representation space of an action-planning Language Model tangibly enhance its physical grounding and its ability to solve unknown tasks?}

Our research attempts to integrate the powerful predictive representations of V-JEPA 2 \citep{assran2025vjepa2} into the procedural framework of Vision-Language World Models (VLWMs) \citep{chen2025planning}. Standard Large Language Models (LLMs) used for task planning are notorious for physical hallucinations—suggesting plans that are linguistically plausible but physically impossible. By default, textual models do not know what an object looks like or how gravity affects it. By tying the textual hidden states of an LLM to the 1024-dimensional V-JEPA latent vectors corresponding to video segments, we hypothesized that the model acts not only as a language engine but as a spatial transition simulator. At inference time, this allows the pipeline to employ a System-2 reranking framework that judges proposed actions based on a latent \textit{energy score}, measuring the deviation between the predicted state and the target goal state vector.

Our architecture reconstructs the logic of proprietary VLWM implementations while adapting it to open-source limitations. A major obstacle in current multimodal planning research is the unavailability of the highly curated Tree-of-Caption datasets used by state-of-the-art works. Treating this constraint as an opportunity to innovate, we explicitly reverse-engineered a synthetic dataset generation pipeline utilizing the raw annotations of the COIN \citep{tang2019coin} and CrossTask \citep{zhukov2019crossTask} video datasets. This pipeline systematically formats raw labels into strictly defined visual state transitions ($\langle A_t, \Delta S_t \rangle$) designed for System-1 trajectory rollout generation, alongside specialized data splits to train our Critic and Goal models.

Through extensive model training across different scales—from 1 billion parameter LoRA models to 8 billion parameter GaLore implementations—our findings demonstrate that when tested on the tasks present within the training distribution, the V-JEPA augmented VLWM recreates the target procedures perfectly, smoothly aligning text and video latent representations. However, we observed a substantial drop-off when applying the model to zero-shot, out-of-distribution tasks. The physical grounding learned by the auxiliary heads struggled to act as a universal physics simulator, reverting instead to linguistic memorization. 

We explicitly analyze these shortcomings, focusing on the challenges of scale (~3,000 videos vs. proprietary millions), the bottlenecks of heuristic energy functions, and the limitations of enforcing continuous video contexts onto discrete text tokens. Overall, the primary contributions of this work are mapping out the intricacies of this hybrid architecture framework, executing a comprehensive synthetic dataset reconstruction, evaluating the stark capabilities and boundaries of test-time search (System-2) scaling in VLMs, and providing a transparent post-mortem on why current V-JEPA text hybridizations struggle with zero-shot spatial generalization.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Procedural Video Understanding:} Early procedural video tasks relied predominantly on dense video captioning and step localization. Benchmarks like COIN \citep{tang2019coin} and CrossTask \citep{zhukov2019crossTask} elevated the field from basic action recognition into sequential reasoning. Recent trends have shifted toward evaluating how well a model can predict future steps temporally, effectively tasking the neural network with planning rather than historical labeling.

\paragraph{Reasoning and Planning in Language Models:} In the NLP domain, planning has been formulated via methodologies like Chain-of-Thought and Tree-of-Thoughts, which allow models to simulate distinct paths before committing to an answer. System-1 and System-2 dual-process theories have been successfully instantiated where a fast language model generates candidate rollouts (System 1) and a deliberate, value-function model scores the logical consistency of those rollouts (System 2). Yet, when applied directly to visual tasks, these models frequently suffer from the ``grounding gap'' simulating steps perfectly aligned textually but drastically unfeasible in real-world application.

\paragraph{Predictive Video Architectures and VLWMs:} The Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) \citep{assran2025vjepa2}, eschews pixel-level generation in favor of predicting abstract representation spaces. The Vision-Language World Model (VLWM) \citep{chen2025planning} builds on this by attempting to merge LLMs and video latents to foresee the consequence of an action. Our work operates directly at the intersection of these domains. We attempt to isolate whether the structural inclusion of V-JEPA via an auxiliary projection head can inject this transition dynamic into openly-available perception architectures.

\section{Architecture and Methodology}

We utilize a dual-system paradigm combining sequential text generation with a test-time search guided by V-JEPA transition distances. 

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
Given a procedural task defined by an initial environmental observation frame $O_0$ (or a textual goal $G$) and an end-goal description, the objective is to generate a viable sequence of actions $\{A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_T\}$ and their expected state changes $\{\Delta S_1, \Delta S_2, \ldots, \Delta S_T\}$. Traditional decoding relies purely on the conditional probability $P(A_t, \Delta S_t \mid O_0, G, A_{<t})$. Our objective updates this by introducing a latent metric evaluation at inference time. 

\subsection{System-1: The Rollout Generator}
System 1 is responsible for the rapid generation of possible future trajectories. We experimented with causal models based on \texttt{facebook/Perception-LM} variants (1B to 8B parameters). 
The model takes as input the overarching task goal description ($G$), an optional visual interpretation mapping $O_0$ into a textual prefix, and the history of preceding actions and state transitions. During inference, we apply high-temperature ($T=0.8$) nucleus sampling to generate $K$ divergent candidate action sequences. 

\subsection{V-JEPA 2 Latent Projection Heads Setup}
The core adaptation involves enforcing video-latent physics directly inside the System-1 model. We utilized a \texttt{vjepa2} ViT-L configuration to process 16-frame slices of original videos, yielding 1024-dimensional feature tensors representing the true physical state change. 

To bridge the LLM text output and the video representations, we attached a 2-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) Projection Head to the final hidden state layers of the System-1 text model. The objective is to map the specific token embeddings describing the state-change ($\Delta S_t$) into the identical 1024-dimensional space as the true V-JEPA observation. During training, the total scalar loss is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{text-NLL} + \alpha \mathcal{L}_{latent}
\end{equation}
$\mathcal{L}_{text-NLL}$ is the standard Negative Log-Likelihood cross-entropy loss. $\mathcal{L}_{latent}$ relies on regression against the pulled V-JEPA vector, forcing the text model's semantic representations to spatially align with the predictive physics engine.

\subsection{System-2: Test-Time Reranking and Goal Critics}
System-1 generations can hallucinate impossible intermediate steps. To counter this, System-2 executes a test-time reranking search. We implemented two distinct evaluators:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Evaluator Critic:} A classification model explicitly trained to predict whether an action sequence is syntactically logical, outputting a real-value scalar, $Cost_{critic}$. 
    \item \textbf{The Goal Latent Evaluator:} For a candidate textual plan, the text is routed through System-1 and the Latent Projection Head to derive a continuous predicted embedding sequence. Specifically, the terminal trajectory latent $z_{pred\_final}$ is generated by aggregating the individual projected latent vectors of each derived action and state-change step:
    \begin{equation}
        z_{pred\_final} = \sum_{t=1}^T z_{pred\_t}
    \end{equation}
    The textual goal itself $G$ is embedded by a separate specialized Goal-Model into $z_{goal}$.
\end{enumerate}

These subsystems collectively output an Energy score $E$, defined as:
\begin{equation}
   E_{total} = \alpha * Cost_{critic} + \beta * \|z_{pred\_final} - z_{goal}\|^2 
\end{equation}
The candidate sequence achieving the lowest energetic deviation is executed. 

\section{Dataset Generation and Synthetic Labeling}
A major research bottleneck was the lack of available data mimicking the proprietary datasets referenced by state-of-the-art models. We developed a robust ingestion pipeline to rebuild comparable structures natively based on the COIN and CrossTask datasets. 

\subsection{Raw Video Processing}
A primary challenge involved widespread link rot across standard dataset URLs. To bypass this, we implemented strict download fallbacks and cached thousands of valid MP4s into a localized storage. Valid videos were temporally split into 16-frame buffers and processed directly through the V-JEPA ViT repository to generate independent foundational tensors. To manage cases where latents couldn't be extracted, the pipeline utilized a randomized tensor fallback to preserve batch integrity during language distribution modeling. 

\subsection{Structuring Target Responses}
We constructed this explicitly formatted JSON structural data directly from the raw textual annotations and temporal bounds native to the COIN and CrossTask datasets. By mapping the raw descriptive strings into precise semantic structures, we formulated definitions for both the action and the immediate resulting physical state consequence. 
To demonstrate the synthesized format explicitly curated for the System-1 multimodal parser, an input/output training sample takes the following configuration:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=json, caption={Sample Dataset Structure formatting State Changes}]
{
  "goal_description": "Change a car tire",
  "history": [
    {"action": "apply parking brake", "state_change": "car is secured"}
  ],
  "next_action": "loosen lug nuts",
  "next_state_change": "nuts are slightly unscrewed but wheel remains on"
}
\end{lstlisting}

Crucially, to cleanly validate generalization to unknown procedural configurations, we enforced severe multi-tier dataset splitting. Standard training regimes frequently bleed structurally identical tasks across subsets, resulting in data leakage. We applied a strict horizontal divide; if a higher-level task existed within the evaluation boundary, none of its step-level variations were exposed to the core training corpus. 

\section{Training Regimen and Hardware Capacity}

Our pipeline executed on an academic hardware cluster. To train architectures up to 8 Billion parameters on singular GPUs, full parameter fine-tuning was fundamentally unachievable. We deployed distinct memory-efficient adaptation layers:
\begin{itemize}[topsep=0pt, leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{1B Parameter Variant via LoRA:} For base tests utilizing a 1B model, we implemented Low-Rank Adaptation ($r=16, \alpha=32$). This significantly accelerated training epochs but ultimately constrained the model's structural capacity to map complex semantic manifolds.
    \item \textbf{8B Parameter Variant via GaLore:} To scale capacity, we utilized Gradient Low-Rank Projection (GaLore) with an orthogonal rank of 256. This allowed near full-parameter update equivalence without exceeding strict memory bounds, effectively avoiding catastrophic forgetting common in standard adaptation methods.
\end{itemize}

During training, we deployed a curriculum strategy: roughly 70\% of batches applied standard textual objective loss, and 30\% applied the latent projection loss to guarantee that linguistic competency stabilized before incorporating physical grounding mechanisms.

\section{Experiments and Analytical Results}

We evaluated our pipeline utilizing a standard task-planning suite structure, testing how models react to varied prompt prefixes and iterative scoring mechanisms.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
Results depend fundamentally on how precisely a system enforces its evaluation matrix:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Task Success Rate (SR):} Checks whether the entire generated sequence is effectively an exact string match for the expected sequence.
    \item \textbf{Step F1 / mAcc (Mean Accuracy):} A step-by-step positional alignment evaluating intermediate matches.
    \item \textbf{mIoU (Mean Intersection over Union):} An unordered similarity tracking whether correct macroscopic events took place even if placed out of sequence.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Generalization Failures at Lower Scales}
Table~\ref{tab:evaluation} surfaces a potent narrative regarding scale constraints. While evaluating on test sets comprised of previously observed training tasks produced near perfect replication execution, attempting completely unknown procedural domains broke smaller networks. 

The 1B model faltered fundamentally. Qualitative inspection generated distinct textual hallucinations—for instance, when prompted to execute \textit{"Paste Car Sticker"} the model outputted sequence logic resembling kitchen recipes rather than automotive maintenance. Moreover, attaching V-JEPA latent projection vectors entirely failed to correct this behavior at the 1B scale. The gradients stabilized the projection layer effectively enough to guess visual states, but they did not reach deep enough into the underlying structural language topology to redirect the hallucination. Consequently, System-2 reranking yielded only a marginal statistical fraction of benefit (e.g., naturally shifting Step F1 from 0.031 to 0.033); sampling multiple candidate rollouts primarily provided varying formats of hallucinated states, rendering the scorer largely ineffective overall. 

\subsection{The Necessity of Scaled Capacity and Search}
Moving to the \texttt{8B GaLore} implementation raised baseline metrics significantly, confirming a necessary minimum capacity threshold requires mapping multimodalities safely. The 8B baseline (Greedy) achieved a Step F1 of 0.128 on completely blind tasks. 

By applying a targeted textual prefix interpreting the visual observation, and utilizing System-2 Reranking Search, capabilities jumped drastically to 0.395 Step F1. The scaled capacity naturally suppressed wide-variance semantic hallucinations. Given sensible base sequences, the Critic and Goal-Latent Energy scorers successfully performed granular differentiation—punishing skipped functional steps and mathematically favoring trajectories closer to the true physical goal latent.

\section{Discussion and Case Analysis}

\subsection{Data Scale Discrepancies}
Our curated dataset utilized thousands of viable mappings against diverse structural hierarchies. However, massive corporate training distributions commonly parse hundreds of thousands of uninterrupted sources. Scale provides a natural topological smoothness. Our results exhibited stark interpolation rigidness strictly because the spatial data bridging completely disassociated physical tasks was too sparse, limiting broad physical analogies.

\subsection{Metric Stringency vs Latent Competency}
One vital qualitative observation involves how rigidly exact-match metrics misrepresent a model's true structural logic. A generated plan frequently comprehends causal physics yet falls victim to semantic variations. Consider a representative rollout captured during the evaluation phase for an unknown maintenance task:

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent
\textbf{Input Goal:} \textit{Change Car Tire} \\
\textbf{Expected Label:} \textit{brake on $\rightarrow$ loosen lug nuts $\rightarrow$ jack up the car} \\
\textbf{Model Generated:} \textit{brake on $\rightarrow$ unscrew wheel lug $\rightarrow$ jack the chassis down}
\vspace{0.5em}

Despite recognizing the correct mechanical tooling (evaluating the progression from brake application to lug interactions and chassis manipulation), the execution output was assigned a nominal F1 score of \textbf{0.000} internally. It deviated from the strict literal requirements \textit{"loosen lug nuts"} and \textit{"jack up"}. This heavily suggests that heuristic metrics fail to accurately reward models for recognizing underlying physics trajectories if the textual domain shifts mildly. Future mechanisms strongly mandate migrating to intelligent semantic-judgement rubrics rather than strict parity checking. 

\section{Conclusion}
This study investigated whether introducing V-JEPA 2 latent physics awareness via an auxiliary projection architecture empowers generic Vision-Language models to cleanly parse procedural tasks. By constructing a custom procedural pipeline mirroring proprietary dataset paradigms, our findings present a multifaceted reality: the architecture flawlessly replicates explicitly trained tasks and physical formats. However, it requires substantive foundational scaling (8B+ parameters) alongside dynamic test-time scoring routines to maintain even partial generalized stability when confronted with unknown zero-shot tasks. 

While fully closing the semantic grounding gap on out-of-distribution structures remains an active challenge—constrained here by dataset volume limitations and heuristic parsing rules—the implementation robustly identifies the systemic thresholds required to merge visual physics directly into standard text generation. These outcomes highlight the exact architectural bottlenecks future methodologies must traverse to enable comprehensive multimodal world simulation.
